How does Mockly compare to Screenshots.pro?

Both make detailed 3D device shots. They differ in where the work runs, how you pay, and how teams share it.

Where the work happens
Mockly: On your Mac. Projects are files you save, and editing and exporting work offline.

Screenshots.pro: On the vendor's service. You upload your files, the render runs there, and you download the finished assets.
Pricing
Mockly: {P_APP@mockly} once, with no subscription. Every editor feature comes with the purchase.

Screenshots.pro: $19 a month Standard or $49 a month Extended, with a discount for annual billing, when we last checked. No one-time option published.
Translation
Mockly: AI Translate sends the text from your Mac to OpenAI, Anthropic, or Google with your own key, and your provider bills you. Or round-trip a CSV with a translator.

Screenshots.pro: Translation runs as part of the vendor's service.
Devices
Mockly: Six 3D models: iPhone 17 Pro Max, iPad Pro 11-inch and 12.9-inch, MacBook Pro 16-inch, Apple Watch Ultra, and Apple Watch Series 11.

Screenshots.pro: 23 devices with 3D angles.
API and teams
Mockly: A single-user Mac app with no API. Projects are files on your Mac, which you share yourself like any other file.

Screenshots.pro: A published REST API and team collaboration.
Upload
Mockly: Upload to App Store Connect with your API key. It replaces the screenshots in that display type for the languages you pick.

Screenshots.pro: Check its site for its current App Store Connect options.

Who should pick Mockly vs Screenshots.pro?

Pick Mockly if…
  • You're an indie developer or small studio who'd rather pay once than carry a subscription
  • You never call a screenshot API and don't need team seats
  • You already pay for OpenAI, Anthropic, or Google and want translation billed there
  • You work alone, and projects saved as files on your Mac suit you
Keep Screenshots.pro if…
  • You need its REST API to generate screenshots from your build pipeline
  • You're on a team that edits screenshot projects together in the cloud
  • You want more device models than Mockly's six
  • You work mostly on computers that aren't Macs
