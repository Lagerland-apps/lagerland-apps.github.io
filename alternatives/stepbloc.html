When should I pick StepBloc instead?

Pick StepBloc if any of these matter more to you than everyday activity tracking, Apple Watch, and the lower lifetime price:

  • You want to do push-ups or squats to unlock. Indoor reps are a different design choice, and StepBloc does them well. EarnLock does not count reps; it counts continuous movement.
  • You need the app on iPad, Mac (Apple Silicon), or Apple Vision Pro. StepBloc ships those platforms; EarnLock is iPhone and Apple Watch only.
  • You want Japanese, Simplified Chinese, or Spanish localizations tuned for those markets.

EarnLock is for the person who tried StepBloc, did the 10 push-ups on the couch in 20 seconds, and decided the cost of unlocking should be steps spread across the day instead of reps fired off in a burst.

What is the best alternative to StepBloc?
EarnLock is the closest direct alternative to StepBloc among screen-time blockers that gate access on physical activity. They differ in how you unlock, where the app runs, and how it looks. EarnLock uses steps, active minutes, and active calories, which build up through the day and are hard to game indoors. StepBloc adds push-ups and squats, rep-based exercises you can finish in front of the same couch you were trying to leave. EarnLock has a first-class Apple Watch app and complications; StepBloc has no Watch app listed. EarnLock's lifetime tier is {P_LIFETIME@earnlock}; StepBloc's is $24.99.
How is EarnLock different from StepBloc?
In four ways. (1) Unlock mechanic: EarnLock uses ambient body movement (steps / active minutes / active calories) read from Apple Health; StepBloc adds rep counting for push-ups and squats. We chose ambient movement over reps on purpose: reps are easy to game at home, while steps and active minutes need continuous motion. (2) Apple Watch: EarnLock ships a first-class Watch app and complications; StepBloc has no Watch app listed. (3) Pricing: EarnLock's lifetime is {P_LIFETIME@earnlock} (one-time); StepBloc's lifetime is $24.99. (4) Privacy and reach: EarnLock's App Privacy label reads Data Not Collected and the app ships in 39 locales; StepBloc collects Health, Fitness, Usage Data, and Diagnostics under Data Not Linked, and ships in 3 locales.

Why does EarnLock use steps instead of push-ups and squats?
Because reps are gameable and steps are continuous. You can do 10 push-ups in 20 seconds without standing up from the couch you were trying to leave. You cannot fake 8,000 steps in a single burst. The count needs sustained motion spread over time, measured by Apple Health, and it builds up from the day's actual movement. EarnLock's premise is that the unlock should cost something that also does you good, and everyday activity does that better than indoor reps. Both apps cite Apple Health for steps; EarnLock additionally supports active minutes and active calories in Premium.
Does StepBloc have an Apple Watch app?
Per StepBloc's App Store listing (developer Hamsters Technology, app ID 6480580480), the app runs on iPhone, iPad, Mac (M1+), and visionOS. No Apple Watch app is listed. EarnLock ships a first-class watchOS app: the Watch reads HealthKit directly so the count keeps running during long walks, runs, and gym sessions without the iPhone in your pocket. Complications come in circular, corner, inline, and rectangular styles.
Which is more private, EarnLock or StepBloc?
EarnLock's Apple App Privacy label reads Data Not Collected. It has no analytics, advertising, or other third-party SDKs, and there is no EarnLock server. StepBloc's App Privacy label lists Health, Fitness, Usage Data (Product Interaction, Other Usage Data), and Diagnostics (Crash Data) under Data Not Linked. Neither app tracks you across other apps. EarnLock is the stricter of the two, since it collects nothing.
