Looking for a
RISE alternative for shift work?

RISE is one of the most popular sleep apps on the App Store, and for people who sleep at roughly the same time each night its sleep-debt and energy forecasts are useful. It has a harder time with shift work, and RISE says so itself. AfterShift starts from your roster: it scores each gap between shifts, counts daytime sleep and naps, and times caffeine to your next start.

How does AfterShift compare to RISE?

RISE is a general sleep and energy app; AfterShift is a narrow tool for one kind of schedule.

What a day looks like to the app
AfterShift: The unit is the window between the end of one shift and the start of the next, whatever the clock says. Seven hours of daytime sleep before a night shift counts the same as seven at night.

RISE: Built around a regular sleep rhythm. Its Melatonin Window and energy schedule assume your body clock has settled into a pattern.
Daytime sleep and naps
AfterShift: Reads daytime sleep from Apple Health like any other sleep, and counts logged naps toward the Shift Recovery Score.

RISE: Its help centre says it doesn't record daytime sleep automatically, so you may see gaps; the fix is logging naps by hand from the Home tab.
Sleep debt and energy
AfterShift: A sleep-debt trend per shift cycle (building, stable, recovering) and a recovery score before each shift. No energy forecast.

RISE: Its best-known features are sleep debt against a sleep need it estimates for you, and an energy schedule predicting your daily peaks and dips. RISE says sleep debt tracking works on any schedule.
Caffeine
AfterShift: A cut-off of 4, 6 or 8 hours before sleep depending on your sensitivity and drink strength, with a reminder timed to your next shift.

RISE: A caffeine cut-off among its habit reminders, timed to your circadian rhythm as RISE estimates it, the same estimate behind the Melatonin Window.
Platforms and devices
AfterShift: iPhone only, with widgets and a Live Activity. No Watch app; reads sleep from any source in Apple Health.

RISE: iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch and Android. Can estimate sleep from your phone alone, or read Apple Health, Fitbit, Oura and others.

RISE: $69.99/year after a 7-day trial; a subscription or trial is needed to use the app. Privacy label lists identifiers as data used to track you.

Who should pick AfterShift vs RISE?

Pick AfterShift if…
  • You work nights, rotations or on-call, and your main sleep often happens in daylight
  • You want naps to count toward your recovery
  • You want caffeine timing tied to your next shift
  • You want a free tier, no account and no tracking
Stick with RISE if…
  • You keep fairly regular hours and like planning your day around energy peaks and dips
  • You want to track sleep with no wearable at all, using your phone
  • You use Android, an iPad or the Apple Watch app
  • You already have months of RISE history and its sleep-debt number works for you

Questions

FAQ

What is the best alternative to RISE for shift workers?
If your sleep keeps moving because of night, rotating or on-call shifts, AfterShift is built for that case. It measures each gap between your shifts, whenever it falls, counts daytime sleep and naps from Apple Health toward a Shift Recovery Score, and times caffeine to your next shift. If you keep regular hours and want a sleep-debt and energy forecast, RISE is the more complete app.
Does RISE work for night shifts?
Partly, by its own account. RISE's help centre says it can still help on overnight or changing schedules and that sleep debt tracking, the Smart Alarm and its relaxation tools work on any schedule. It also says the Melatonin Window may not be a reliable bedtime guide if your schedule rotates, and that RISE doesn't record daytime sleep automatically, so you may need to log naps by hand.
How is AfterShift different from RISE?
RISE predicts your energy peaks and dips from your circadian rhythm and tracks sleep debt against a sleep need it estimates for you. AfterShift starts from your roster: it measures the window between the end of one shift and the start of the next, scores it on sleep amount, continuity and stages with naps included, caps the score when the gap is too short for a full sleep or you are on call, and plans the next sleep window and caffeine cut-off around the shift ahead.
How much do RISE and AfterShift cost?
RISE Premium is $69.99 a year on the U.S. App Store after a 7-day free trial, and its listing says a subscription or trial is required to access app content. AfterShift is free to download with core sleep and nap tracking, calendar shift import and caffeine reminders; Premium is {P_MONTHLY@aftershift} a month, {P_YEARLY@aftershift} a year, or {P_LIFETIME@aftershift} once for lifetime.
Do I need an Apple Watch for either app?
No. RISE can estimate sleep from your phone and also reads Apple Health, Apple Watch, Fitbit, Oura and other trackers, and it has its own Apple Watch app. AfterShift has no Watch app; it reads sleep from any app or device that writes to Apple Health and lets you log naps by hand. A tracker that records sleep stages adds detail to AfterShift's score but isn't required.
What about privacy?
AfterShift has no account, no analytics SDKs and no cloud sync; your data stays on your device. RISE's App Store privacy label lists identifiers as data used to track you, and health and fitness, contact info, identifiers, usage data and diagnostics as data linked to you.
