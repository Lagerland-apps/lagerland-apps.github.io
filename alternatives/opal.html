When should I pick Opal instead?

Opal does more than EarnLock in several places. Pick it if one of these matters most:

  • Website blocking is high on your list. Opal's listing names it as a feature next to app blocking.
  • You think in focus sessions ("block Instagram for the next two hours while I work") more than in a daily goal, and you want them synced with iPhone Focus modes.
  • You don't want movement anywhere near your screen time. EarnLock's key is always an activity goal from Apple Health.
  • You like Opal's block screens, reports, and streaks, and a subscription is fine with you.

EarnLock suits someone who has ended one focus session too many and wants the key to be a walk instead of a tap. A walk opens the apps, a slow day still earns an hour at halfway, and the first week shows you how often you reach for them before anything is blocked.

What is the best alternative to Opal?
If you want movement to be the key, EarnLock is the Opal alternative built around it. It locks the apps you pick until you reach a daily activity goal measured by Apple Health: halfway opens them for an hour, and the full goal opens them until midnight. It starts with a Shadow Week that blocks nothing and counts how often you would have been blocked. Locking one app is free for good, there is no account, and Premium is available monthly, yearly, or as a one-time {P_LIFETIME@earnlock} purchase. If focus sessions or website blocking are what you need most, Opal is the better fit.
How is EarnLock different from Opal?
Opal blocks apps and websites on schedules and focus sessions, and you decide when a session ends. EarnLock unlocks only when Apple Health shows you've moved enough that day, with a math-gated emergency unlock once a day for real needs. A locked app shows exactly what's left, such as "2,026 steps until unlock". EarnLock also offers a one-time Lifetime purchase, while Opal's listing shows subscriptions only. Opal is broader: websites, focus sessions synced with Focus modes, and reports.

Does EarnLock work on Apple Watch?
Yes, with Premium. With Premium, EarnLock's Apple Watch app reads its own Health data and shows your ring, your count, and how far you are from unlocking, with a Reflect button and circular, corner, inline, and rectangular complications. The iPhone is still the device that locks and unlocks your apps, and it passes your Premium status to the watch. Opal's listing says it is for iPhone only.
Is EarnLock private?
Yes. EarnLock has no account, makes no network connections of its own, and includes no analytics, advertising, or other third-party SDKs. Apple Health is read on your iPhone and never written to or sent anywhere, and Apple's Screen Time picker gives EarnLock opaque tokens instead of the names of your apps. EarnLock is built for limiting your own iPhone and has no parent controls.
Can EarnLock block the same apps Opal can?
For apps, yes. Both use Apple's Screen Time controls, so you choose apps or whole categories in the same iOS-provided picker. The free tier locks one app or category, and Premium has no limit. Opal also lists website blocking as a feature; EarnLock is built around apps and categories.
