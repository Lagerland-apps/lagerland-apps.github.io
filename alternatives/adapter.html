FAQ

What is a good Adapter alternative for Mac?
MediaKit runs on Mac and iPhone and covers Adapter's video, audio, and image conversions, plus PDF, archive, and ebook tools, more than 150 in all. Drop a file in and MediaKit suggests the tools that fit it. Every tool runs on your device.
Adapter is free, so why choose MediaKit?
If a free converter is all you need, Adapter is a solid choice. MediaKit keeps five tools free for good (Video Compress, Image Compress, Image Resize, Audio Convert, and PDF Compress) and charges for the rest. With Pro you get every tool, batch processing in the Queue, Recipes that save a chain of steps, and Make It Fit for a target size or platform, on iPhone as well as Mac.
Does MediaKit support the formats Adapter handles?
For everyday work, mostly. MediaKit compresses, converts, trims, and resizes video and opens MKV directly. It writes audio as M4A (AAC), WAV, AIFF, ALAC, or CAF from formats including MP3 and FLAC, and converts images to HEIC, JPG, PNG, TIFF, GIF, and WebP where your system can write it. It does not support WebM and cannot write MP3, which FFmpeg-based tools like Adapter can.
Is MediaKit on-device like Adapter?
Yes. Both convert locally and upload nothing. MediaKit makes no network requests of its own, needs no account, and has no analytics SDK. Its Privacy Manifest is on the transparency page.
Which platforms does MediaKit run on versus Adapter?
MediaKit runs on iPhone and Mac, and one purchase covers both. There is no iPad or Windows version. Adapter is a desktop converter for Mac and Windows. If you need Windows, pick Adapter. If you want the same tools on your iPhone, pick MediaKit.
How much does MediaKit cost?
