A Chessly alternative
built from the games you've actually played.

Chessly is GothamChess's course platform, where Levy Rozman and other titled players walk you through opening repertoires on video. The real test comes in your own games, when an opponent plays a move the course didn't cover. Chessful's Opening Repertoire reads your analysed games and shows which openings you really play, how you score in each, and where you leave the book. Stockfish checks the rest of the game on your device, and each mistake is explained in plain language and comes back in your daily training.

How does Chessful compare to Chessly?

They cover different parts of getting better at chess.

Training source
Chessful: Today's Training builds a session from the mistakes in your analysed games, and spaced repetition brings misses back.

Chessly: Opening-repertoire video courses by Levy Rozman (GothamChess) and other titled players, taught as a curriculum.
Lessons
Chessful: 156 interactive lessons, all free, from the board itself to strategy. No video.

Chessly: Video courses presented by GothamChess and other titled players.
Openings
Chessful: Opening Repertoire shows your most-played openings, your score in each, and where you leave the book. Premium adds the full tree, engine corrections, and line drills.

Chessly: Openings primarily, with some middlegame and endgame coverage in specific courses.

Account requirement
Chessful: No account.

Questions

FAQ

What is the best Chessly alternative for iPhone?
Chessful suits a player who already has an opening and wants to see how it goes in real games. Chessly sells opening-repertoire video courses by Levy Rozman (GothamChess) and other titled players. Chessful analyses your games with Stockfish on your iPhone, iPad, or Mac, shows where you leave the book in Opening Repertoire, explains each mistake in plain language, and builds your daily training from those mistakes. They work well together: Chessly for the opening curriculum, Chessful for what happens once the game leaves it.
Does Chessful have video opening courses like Chessly?
No. Chessful has no video courses and no coach-presented curriculum. It has 156 interactive lessons, free, that start with the board itself and include sections on openings, endgames, and strategy. Its training comes from the mistakes in your own games, each tagged with one of 10 types such as Missed Fork, Poor King Safety, or Opening Inaccuracy.
How much does Chessful cost compared to Chessly?
Chessful is free with daily limits: 2 analyses, 3 puzzles, and 2 coach hints a day, the first 10 opponents, and all 156 lessons. Premium removes the limits for {P_MONTHLY@chessful}/month or {P_YEARLY@chessful}/year, each with a {TRIAL@chessful}, or {P_LIFETIME@chessful} once. Chessly sells video courses with a paid membership; check Chessly for current prices.
Can I use both Chessly and Chessful?
Yes. Chessly is an opening curriculum: you work through a structured repertoire with a strong coach. Chessful covers the rest of the game you actually played, from hanging pieces and missed forks to king safety and endgame errors. Import your games from Chess.com or Lichess as PGN, and Chessful turns their mistakes into training.
Does Chessful check the openings I play?
Yes. Opening Repertoire reads your analysed games and shows the openings you play most, how you score in each, and where you leave the book. Premium adds the full move tree, engine corrections, and drills for the lines you keep getting wrong. Chessful tracks the repertoire you actually play, while Chessly teaches you a recommended repertoire from scratch.
