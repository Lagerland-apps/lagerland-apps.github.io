How does MediaKit compare to HandBrake?

HandBrake is an excellent transcoder that does one job. MediaKit is a wider toolbox for iPhone & Mac.

Scope
MediaKit: 152 tools covering video, audio, image, PDF, and archive work.

HandBrake: A video and audio transcoder that goes deep on that one job.
Encoder depth
MediaKit: Profiles for common cases plus a detailed config view, for when you just want this clip smaller.

HandBrake: Full x264, x265, and AV1 controls, with every setting exposed for power users.
Platform & UI
MediaKit: Native on iPhone and Mac (one Universal Purchase), with modern drag-and-drop and a unified inspector.

HandBrake: Desktop-only (Windows/Mac/Linux). A power-tool UI with several inspector tabs, great once you know it.
Privacy / network
MediaKit: No network calls and no upload. See the Privacy Manifest.

HandBrake: Local and open-source, so also on-device.

Who should pick MediaKit vs HandBrake?

Pick MediaKit if…
  • You do video and audio and image and PDF and archive work in the same week
  • You're tired of uploading files to web converters
  • You want a modern UI with a unified inspector on iPhone and Mac
  • Common-case encoding presets cover 90% of your work
  • You want all of that in one app
Stick with HandBrake if…
  • You're a power encoder who tunes RF, B-frames, and refs by hand
  • You batch-encode movie libraries with custom CLI workflows
  • You only need video transcoding and prefer a focused open-source tool
  • You contribute to or extend HandBrake yourself

Questions

FAQ

What is the best HandBrake alternative for Mac?
MediaKit is a native iPhone and Mac alternative to HandBrake with a wider toolset. It bundles 152 local tools for video transcoding, audio conversion, image compression, PDF tasks, and archive work behind a single drag-and-drop interface. Everything runs on-device, and no file ever leaves it.
Does MediaKit upload my files to a server?
No. MediaKit is 100% on-device. It exists so you don't have to upload your file to a random website to convert it, which has become the default on the open web.
Does MediaKit run on iPhone too?
Yes. MediaKit is a Universal Purchase: buy it once and use it natively on both iPhone and Mac. HandBrake is desktop-only (Windows, Mac, and Linux), so MediaKit has the edge if you also want to convert files on your iPhone.
Does MediaKit support the formats HandBrake handles?
For the common cases, yes. MediaKit handles H.264, HEVC, AV1, audio extraction, frame-rate changes, and resizing for video. HandBrake's encoder tuning goes deeper for niche cases, while MediaKit also covers audio, image, PDF, and archive work that HandBrake doesn't do.
What does MediaKit cost?
MediaKit is free to start. Some advanced tools and batch operations are part of a Pro tier, but the core conversion workflows are available without payment.
