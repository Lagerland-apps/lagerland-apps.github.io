Kiwix is the app for true offline Wikipedia. Download a ZIM archive and you can read the entire encyclopedia with no connection, which is hard to beat for travel, remote areas, and places without internet. WanderWiki does a different job. It turns the live Wikipedia API into a swipeable discovery feed with no ads and no account, and you can save individual articles for offline reading. The two work well side by side.

How does WanderWiki compare to Kiwix?

Kiwix is a downloaded archive, and WanderWiki is a live discovery feed.

Core purpose
WanderWiki: Discovery. You swipe a deck of random, topic-based, and Today in History articles, and Where next offers three related articles under each one you read.

Kiwix: Offline reference, a full downloaded encyclopedia you can search anywhere.
Offline model
WanderWiki: Live API; save individual articles for offline reading.

Kiwix: Full ZIM archive on-device, so everything works offline.
Storage
WanderWiki: Light, only the articles you save.

Kiwix: Heavy, since archives are often several gigabytes.

Kiwix: Free and open-source.

Who should pick WanderWiki vs Kiwix?

Pick WanderWiki if…
  • You want a handful of new articles to read each day on your phone
  • You want an ad-free swipe deck with Today in History and topic feeds
  • You only need to save a handful of articles for offline reading
  • You want a light app without a multi-gigabyte archive
  • You'd like articles read aloud, a reader that remembers where you stopped, and today's history on a widget
Use Kiwix if…
  • You need the entire encyclopedia offline, for travel, remote areas, or no data
  • You want full offline search across all of Wikipedia
  • You're equipping a device for low- or no-connectivity environments
  • You want a free, open-source archive reader

The two fit side by side: Kiwix for real offline situations, WanderWiki for everyday browsing.

Questions

FAQ

Is WanderWiki a replacement for Kiwix?
No. Kiwix is the standard app for true offline Wikipedia: you download a full ZIM archive and read the whole encyclopedia with no connection at all. WanderWiki is a reader for discovering articles on the live Wikipedia API, a swipeable deck for browsing where you can save individual articles for offline reading. If you need the entire encyclopedia offline, Kiwix is the right tool.
What is WanderWiki good for that Kiwix isn't?
Finding articles you weren't looking for. Kiwix is for looking things up in a downloaded archive. WanderWiki gives you a swipeable feed of random articles, topic decks, Today in History, and a For You mix of the topics you pick, with no ads and no account. Use it when you want Wikipedia to surprise you; when you need a specific article offline on a plane, use Kiwix.
Can WanderWiki read Wikipedia fully offline like Kiwix?
Not in the same way. Kiwix stores an entire offline ZIM archive (often several gigabytes) so everything is available with no connection. WanderWiki fetches live articles from Wikipedia's API and downloads the full text of the articles you save, so those open offline. That works for a reading list, but it is not a full offline encyclopedia.
Is WanderWiki free like Kiwix?
Kiwix is free and open-source. WanderWiki is free for 20 articles a day; Premium, which adds For You, Today in History, and unlimited swiping, is {P_MONTHLY@wanderwiki}/month, {P_YEARLY@wanderwiki}/year, or {P_LIFETIME@wanderwiki} once. No plan shows ads. The two apps serve different needs, and their pricing differs too.
Does WanderWiki use real Wikipedia content?
Yes. WanderWiki uses Wikipedia's public API to fetch real articles, the same content Kiwix archives, in 32 Wikipedia language editions, with no ads and no account. It presents them as a live discovery deck, where Kiwix gives you a downloaded archive.
Should I use both Kiwix and WanderWiki?
That works well. Keep Kiwix for real offline situations such as travel without data, remote fieldwork, or emergencies, and use WanderWiki day to day for ad-free browsing on your iPhone or iPad. One is for looking things up offline, the other for reading whatever turns up.
