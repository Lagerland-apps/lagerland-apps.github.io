When should I pick one sec instead?

Pick one sec if any of these matter more to you than an enforced block that only movement lifts:

  • The breathing pause is enough for you. For many people a moment of friction is all it takes to close the app, and that is a gentler approach than a hard block.
  • You want the range of interruptions one sec offers (breathe, reflect, wait) over a single activity requirement.
  • You need Android as well as iOS. one sec is cross-platform; EarnLock is iOS + Apple Watch only.
  • You don't want activity goals tied to movement (EarnLock's free tier needs step data from Apple Health).

EarnLock is for someone who has tried pause-based blockers, tapped through every time, and wants unlocking to cost some physical effort.

What is the best alternative to one sec?
EarnLock is a privacy-first, movement-based alternative to one sec. one sec adds a breathing pause before you open a distracting app, then lets you proceed if you still want to. EarnLock shields your chosen apps through Apple's Family Controls until you reach a daily activity goal in steps, active minutes, or active energy, measured by Apple Health. The custom shield shows a live progress ring with the exact count remaining. It offers a {P_LIFETIME@earnlock} one-time lifetime option and has no account, no analytics SDK and no server.
How is EarnLock different from one sec?
one sec uses intentional friction: a deep breath or a short delay before the app opens, after which you can still continue. EarnLock enforces a block: the app stays shielded until you reach a daily activity goal measured by Apple Health, so you unlock it by moving instead of tapping through. EarnLock also has a full Apple Watch app. one sec works on iOS and Android; EarnLock is iOS and Apple Watch only.
Is EarnLock cheaper than one sec?
Generally yes on the lifetime tier. EarnLock is {P_LIFETIME@earnlock} lifetime (one-time). one sec offers a free tier for a single app and a Pro plan billed monthly or yearly, and its lifetime option has risen a lot over time (well above EarnLock's). Both are free to start; they differ in what the paid tier costs and what it takes to unlock an app. Check current prices before switching.
Does one sec block apps or just pause them?
one sec is built around friction, without a hard block. It interrupts the app launch with a breathing exercise or short delay and then asks whether you still want to continue. Many people close the app at that point, and that is what the design is good at. EarnLock is a hard block: the app cannot be opened until your daily activity goal is met, apart from one math-gated emergency unlock per day for real emergencies.
Is EarnLock private?
Yes. EarnLock has no account, no server, and no third-party SDKs of any kind, analytics and advertising included. HealthKit data is read on-device only and never leaves the device. Apple's App Privacy nutrition label shows Data Not Collected for every release. EarnLock is for restricting yourself and is never a parental control.
Can EarnLock block the same apps one sec can?
Yes. Both apps build on Apple's Family Controls / Screen Time APIs, so the picker that selects which apps to gate is the same iOS-provided UI. Any app or category one sec can intercept, EarnLock can shield. The difference is that EarnLock keeps it locked until you move, where one sec pauses and then lets you through.
