The official Wikipedia app is excellent if you know what you're looking up. WanderWiki is for the other 80% of the time, when you want Wikipedia as a feed of interesting things you didn't know you wanted to know. How does WanderWiki compare to the Wikipedia app?

Both show the same articles; they differ in how you read them.

Entry mode
WanderWiki: Discovery-first. Random articles, topic decks, Today in History.

Wikipedia app: Search-first reference, with a featured-content surface.
Interaction
WanderWiki: Swipe through cards like a deck. Move on or read deeper.

Wikipedia app: Tap into an article, then back out. List/grid navigation.
Offline + saving
WanderWiki: Save articles offline; the saved list is local.

Wikipedia app: Saved articles via Reading Lists; sync via Wikipedia account.
Privacy
WanderWiki: No account, no SDKs. Requests go only to Wikipedia and its image server. See the Privacy Manifest.

Wikipedia app: Optional account for cross-device sync.

Who should pick WanderWiki vs the Wikipedia app?

Pick WanderWiki if…
  • You open Wikipedia to be surprised more than to look something up
  • You want a swipe-deck reading mode instead of a search box
  • You like Today in History and want it surfaced daily
  • You read on commutes or planes and want offline articles ready
  • You'd rather not have a Wikipedia account or feed-style ranking
Stick with the Wikipedia app if…
  • You use Wikipedia as a reference and search-first is the right entry point
  • You contribute or edit and need the editorial tooling
  • You rely on cross-device Reading List sync via a Wikipedia account
  • You want the official featured / current-events surface

FAQ

What is a good Wikipedia app alternative for browsing rather than searching?
WanderWiki is a discovery-first iPhone and iPad alternative to the official Wikipedia app. Where the official app is a search-first reference, WanderWiki shows Wikipedia as a swipeable deck of articles, with random discovery, personalised topics, Today in History, and offline reading. It has no ads, no algorithms, and no account.
Does WanderWiki use real Wikipedia content?
Yes. WanderWiki uses Wikipedia's public API to fetch real articles, the same content the official app shows. Only the presentation differs: swipeable cards, personalised topics, and Today in History, where the official app leads with search.
Can I save articles for offline reading in WanderWiki?
Yes. You can save articles you swiped past and read them later without a connection. Saved articles are stored locally on your device.
Does WanderWiki track me?
No. WanderWiki has no third-party tracking or advertising SDKs and needs no account. Article requests go to Wikipedia's public API the same way a browser visit would.
