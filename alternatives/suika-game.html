Fixed boards · Visible queue · A proven answer

A Suika Game alternative
that trades the tumble for a solution.

Suika Game has earned its fans: drop a fruit, watch it roll, hold your breath while a merge shoves a grape toward the line. The chaos and the near-misses are the charm, and there is no single right answer — each run is a fresh chase for a higher score and the watermelon. Millrace is a different kind of merge game. The board is fixed, the whole queue of arriving tiles is on screen, and each level asks for one exact value on one marked cell within a move budget. You can reason a level all the way through, and when you solve it, the game tells you whether a shorter solution existed.

How does Millrace compare to Suika Game?

Both are merge games. They pose different puzzles — here is what you actually do in each.

What you do each turn
Millrace: Swipe, and every tile on the 4×4 grid slides that way; matching numbers that collide combine into one. Then the next tile in the queue lands on the inlet, a marked cell on the edge of the board.

Suika Game: Move the dropper along the top of the box and let go. The fruit falls, rolls and settles; two fruits of the same kind that touch merge into the next size up.
What you are chasing
Millrace: One exact value — say, exactly 64 — on one marked cell, the vault, before the move budget runs out. The level is solved or it isn't.

Suika Game: A high score, and the watermelon at the top of an eleven-fruit ladder that starts at a cherry.
Luck and what you can see
Millrace: Nothing random in the campaign. The starting board is fixed and the whole arrival queue is on screen before it lands, so you can reason a level all the way through.

Suika Game: You are handed one of the five smallest fruits at random, with the next one shown. Bouncy physics mean a settled pile can shift or a merge can launch a fruit — and that unpredictability is much of the fun.
How you lose
Millrace: Two ways, both your own doing: slide a tile over the inlet so the next arrival has nowhere to land, or merge past the target — a 128 when the level asked for 64 — which cannot be rebuilt.

Suika Game: A fruit goes over the top of the box. That includes one knocked upward by a merge, which is where the near-misses come from.
Structure and progress
Millrace: 64 levels in six chapters, each pinned to a known shortest solution — five moves in Warm-up, up to ten or more in Mastery. Solve one and the result card shows your moves and, if you took the long way, the best possible. Freeplay adds an endless board, a daily board shared by every player, and streaks.

Suika Game: No levels or stages — one endless run at a time, with online rankings and your best scores kept. Skins and screen decorations change the look.
Price, ads & privacy
Millrace: Free — the whole game, including all 64 levels, Freeplay, Hint and the Coach. Optional theme packs and undo bundles; no ads, no subscription. Privacy label: Data Not Collected, and it works fully offline.

Suika Game: $2.99 with optional skins; privacy label Data Not Linked to You. Suika Game+ on Apple Arcade has no ads or in-app purchases and a Data Not Collected label.

Questions

FAQ

What is a good Suika Game alternative on iPhone?
Millrace, if what you want is a merge game with a problem to solve rather than a physics tumble. Each of its 64 levels gives you a fixed 4×4 board, the whole queue of arriving tiles, and one exact value to deliver to one marked cell within a move budget. Nothing in a level is random, and every level has a proven shortest solution. It is free with no ads, and an endless Freeplay board covers pure score play. If the physics and the watermelon chase are what you love, the official Suika Game app is still the one to play.
How is Millrace different from Suika Game?
Suika Game is a physics drop-and-merge game: you drop fruit into a box, two fruits of the same kind that touch merge into the next size up, and the run ends when a fruit goes over the top. Millrace is a tile-delivery puzzle: each swipe slides every tile on a 4×4 grid, matching numbers combine, and after each move the next tile from a visible queue lands on a marked inlet. Suika Game asks for the highest score you can reach. Millrace asks for one exact value on one marked cell, and you lose the level if you block the inlet or merge past the target.
Is Suika Game luck or skill, and is Millrace random?
Suika Game is both. Where you drop matters a great deal, but the fruit you are handed is random (you can see one ahead) and the physics are bouncy, so a settled pile can shift and a merge can launch a fruit over the line. That unpredictability is a large part of its charm. Millrace levels have no randomness at all: the starting board is fixed and the arrival queue is authored and fully visible, so every position follows from the moves you play. Freeplay, the endless mode, is the one place where new tiles appear at random.
Is there an official Suika Game on iPhone?
Yes. Suika Game-Aladdin X is the official iPhone and iPad release, sold on the App Store for $2.99 with optional skin purchases. Suika Game+ is the Apple Arcade edition, with no ads, no in-app purchases and Game Center leaderboards, and a sequel, Suika Game Planet, drops fruit around a circular stage. The game began on Aladdin X projectors and became a hit on Nintendo Switch. The official apps are listed under XGIMI LIMITED; many free fruit-merge games on the App Store borrow the idea but are not the official one.
Does Millrace have an endless mode like Suika Game?
Yes — Freeplay, one tap from the level list. It is an endless slide-and-merge board with no goal and no move limit, with personal bests, a daily board that is identical for every player in the world, and daily streaks. It covers the score-chasing itch, but it is not a physics game: tiles slide on a grid rather than tumbling in a box. The 64-level campaign is the heart of Millrace.
How much does Millrace cost compared with Suika Game?
Millrace is free, and so is the whole game — all 64 levels, Freeplay, the daily board, Hint and the Coach. The optional purchases are theme packs at $1.99 each (or $5.99 for all sixteen paid themes) and undo bundles from $0.99; there are no ads and no subscription, and no level ever needs an undo. Suika Game-Aladdin X costs $2.99 up front with optional skins, and Suika Game+ is included with an Apple Arcade subscription.
