App Store revenue · No SDK · Apple's own reports

Looking at RevenueCat
just for the charts?

RevenueCat is very good at what it is built for. Its SDK and backend handle purchases, entitlements, paywalls and experiments across stores, and its free RevenueCat Mobile app, rated 4.9 from about 1,600 ratings, puts charts, customer history and purchase notifications on your phone. If you want that system running your subscriptions, use it. Some developers only want to see their App Store numbers, though, and don't want a purchase backend for that. AppMeta Pulse is for them: it reads the reports Apple already publishes, with an API key, and nothing goes into your app.

How does AppMeta Pulse compare to RevenueCat?

RevenueCat records purchases as they happen, through the path you route them along. Pulse reads Apple's reports afterwards. Most of the differences below follow from that one choice.

What it is
AppMeta Pulse: A dashboard app for App Store Connect. It reads Apple's Sales and Trends, Subscription and Subscription Event reports, App Store impressions and page views, and customer reviews.

RevenueCat: An in-app purchase platform: SDKs, a backend, entitlements, paywalls, A/B experiments, integrations, a web dashboard with charts, and the RevenueCat Mobile app.

RevenueCat does much more. Pulse covers only the reading part.
Setup
AppMeta Pulse: Create an App Store Connect API key with the Admin role, add your Vendor Number, done. No SDK, no code change, no server notification URL, no new release of your app.

RevenueCat: Integrate the SDK, or connect the store and turn on tracking of new purchases from App Store server notifications, which RevenueCat documents as its SDK-less route. Purchases made before setup need a receipt import.

Pulse also works for apps that never touched RevenueCat, including paid apps and old projects.
Which numbers you see
AppMeta Pulse: Apple's own terms. Proceeds after commission and tax by default, or Sales with one setting; MRR from Apple's Subscription report; trials, and downloads net of refunds. The figures are next-day, because Apple publishes daily reports the following day.

RevenueCat: RevenueCat's metrics: active trials, active subscriptions, MRR, revenue, new and active customers, updated in near real time. The Overview's revenue card is gross, before store fees and taxes.

Expect the two to differ. They measure at different points and on different bases.
Stores and customers
AppMeta Pulse: App Store only, for every app in your App Store Connect account. Apple's reports are aggregated, so there is no per-customer history.

RevenueCat: App Store, Google Play, Stripe and other stores in one place, with each customer's purchase history.

For a cross-platform subscription business, RevenueCat is the stronger tool.
On the phone
AppMeta Pulse: 12 Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets, review replies with optional on-device AI drafts, iCloud Sync between your devices. No push notifications.

RevenueCat: RevenueCat Mobile: charts, customer history, purchase notifications, widgets, Siri and Shortcuts, the Rico AI assistant and an AI paywall editor. iPhone, iPad, Apple silicon Mac and Vision Pro, iOS 18 or later.

If you want a ping for every purchase, RevenueCat Mobile does that and Pulse doesn't.
Price and privacy
AppMeta Pulse: Free trial, then from $0.99 a month. Privacy label: Data Not Collected. App Store Connect requests go from your device straight to Apple; nothing passes through a Lagerland server.

RevenueCat: Free up to $2,500 in monthly tracked revenue, then 1% of tracked revenue; RevenueCat Mobile is free. The mobile app's privacy label lists contact info and usage data linked to you. The service itself processes your customers' purchases, which is its job.

Under $2,500 a month RevenueCat can cost nothing. Check current pricing on both sides.

Questions

FAQ

Is AppMeta Pulse a RevenueCat alternative?
Only for the dashboard part. RevenueCat is an in-app purchase backend with SDKs, entitlements, paywalls, experiments, integrations and charts across several stores. AppMeta Pulse is an App Store Connect dashboard that reads Apple's own reports. If you use RevenueCat to run your purchases, keep it. If you were considering it only to see App Store revenue and subscriptions, Pulse does that without adding anything to your app.
Do I need to add an SDK to use AppMeta Pulse?
No. AppMeta Pulse reads Apple's Sales and Trends, Subscription and Subscription Event reports through the App Store Connect API, using a key with the Admin role that you create in App Store Connect. Nothing changes in your app, and it works for every app in the account, including paid apps and apps you no longer update.
Can RevenueCat work without its SDK?
Yes. RevenueCat documents an SDK-less integration: you connect the store, set up App Store server notifications and turn on tracking of new purchases, or send receipts to its REST API. It tracks purchases from the point it starts receiving them; purchases from before that need a receipt import.
Why do RevenueCat and AppMeta Pulse show different revenue?
They measure at different points. RevenueCat's Overview shows gross revenue before store fees and taxes, updated in near real time from the purchases it receives. AppMeta Pulse shows Apple's proceeds after commission and tax by default, or sales, from Apple's daily reports, which arrive the following day. Exchange rates and refund timing differ too.
Does AppMeta Pulse work with Google Play or Stripe?
No. AppMeta Pulse reads App Store Connect only. If you sell subscriptions on Google Play, Stripe or the web as well, RevenueCat's cross-store view is the better fit.
How much do they cost?
RevenueCat is free up to $2,500 in monthly tracked revenue, then charges 1% of tracked revenue, and the RevenueCat Mobile app is free for any RevenueCat account. AppMeta Pulse has a free trial, then costs from $0.99 a month. Check current pricing on both sides before deciding.
