What you copy can be anything: an address, a message, a code, a photo. So Clippet is built to keep it with you. It has no account system, no server of its own, no analytics and no advertising, and it contains no third-party code. This policy explains what Clippet stores, where it stores it, the few moments something leaves your device, and the choices you have. data_collection: none data_storage: > Clippet stores your clips on your device: their content (text, links, images, videos, PDFs, audio, files and code), thumbnails, link previews, the names, tags and boards you give them, custom actions, auto-delete times, whether a clip holds a card number, how it arrived (a copy, a drop, the share sheet), and on the Mac the app you copied it from. They live in a database inside the app's own container on the device, which Clippet's share extension, action extension, keyboard and widgets also read. Your settings, your last five searches and Clippet Pro's status are kept on the device too. Temporary files for previews, drags and shares are deleted once they are no longer needed. Clippet does not use the Keychain. Deleting the app removes this data from that device. If you back up your device to iCloud or a computer, Clippet's data may be included in that backup under your Apple settings. icloud_sync: - > iCloud Sync is on by default and can be turned off in Settings › iCloud Sync & Backup. While it is on, your clips, boards, tags, names, custom actions, auto-delete times and two settings (Keep History and Auto-Delete Sensitive Clips) are stored in your own private iCloud database through Apple's CloudKit, so they reach your other devices signed in to the same Apple Account. Apple stores this data under its own privacy policy. Lagerland Apps has no access to your private iCloud database and cannot read, copy or delete what is in it. - > Clips saved through the share sheet, the Save to Clippet action or the keyboard's Save button are uploaded the next time Clippet runs. A deletion, including an auto-delete, syncs to every device. third_parties: > Clippet contains no third-party analytics, advertising, attribution, crash-reporting or tracking code, and its privacy manifests declare no tracking, no tracking domains and no collected data. Apart from your own iCloud, data leaves your device in these cases only. Link previews: when you save a web link, Clippet asks that page for its title, icon and image, the way a browser would. The website sees the request and your IP address; the request carries only the link. Clippet never does this for local or private addresses, links with a user name, or links that carry a password, token, key or similar code, and it stops if a redirect leads to one. Turn off Settings › Capture › Fetch Link Previews and Clippet never contacts a copied link's website. Actions you tap: a Smart Action can open the clip's own link, Apple Maps for an address, a Google search for a flight or a web search, or a carrier's tracking page (UPS, FedEx, USPS or DHL) for a parcel. These open only when you tap them, and that website's own privacy practices then apply. On the Mac, if the built-in translation cannot translate a clip, Clippet offers to open Google Translate and tells you first that up to 2,000 characters of the text will be sent to Google. On iPhone and iPad, translation uses Apple's translation sheet. Purchases: Clippet Pro is sold through the App Store. Apple handles the payment, the receipt and Restore Purchases under its own privacy policy; Clippet only asks the App Store which plan your Apple Account holds and never sees your payment details. What you share: a clip you copy, drag, share or back up goes only where you send it. your_choices: - > You decide what Clippet keeps. Settings › Capture turns automatic saving on or off, and Types to Save turns each kind of clip on or off. On iPhone and iPad, iOS asks before Clippet reads a copy from another app, and the Paste from Other Apps setting (Ask, Deny or Allow) on Clippet's page in the Settings app is yours to change. - > You decide how long it keeps it. Delete any clip, set a clip to delete itself after a time or on a date, or let Keep History remove clips you have not used in 24 hours, 7 days or 30 days. Pinned clips and clips on a board are kept until you remove them. Deletions sync to all your devices while iCloud Sync is on. - > Create Backup saves your clips, boards and tags to a file in a place you choose. The file is not encrypted, so Clippet leaves out clips that hold a card number and clips past their auto-delete time. Store the file somewhere you trust. - > App Lock puts Clippet behind Face ID, Touch ID, Optic ID or your device passcode. The system handles the check; Clippet learns only whether it passed. While App Lock is on, the widgets show no clips and the keyboard follows the lock. your_rights: - > Lagerland Apps does not hold any of your personal data: your clips stay on your devices and in your own iCloud, which only you control. You can see, back up, rename and delete all of it in the app at any time. A clip you delete while iCloud Sync is on is removed from your iCloud too, and your device's iCloud storage settings let you remove what Clippet keeps there. If you write to us, we use your email only to answer you. You can ask us to delete that correspondence, and you have the right to complain to your data protection authority. sensitive_data: > Clippet does not try to keep passwords. A copy that its source app marks as concealed, transient or auto-generated, as password managers mark the passwords they copy, is skipped before anything in it is read. This works only for apps that mark their copies. When Clippet recognizes a payment card number in text, a link, code or an image, it flags the clip and offers to delete it after 30 minutes (or another time you set); nothing is deleted until you tap Enable. The widgets, the keyboard and the Mac's menu bar and Quick Paste show "Card number hidden" instead of the number. When you copy a card number or a one-time code back out of Clippet, it is marked as concealed, kept off Universal Clipboard and cleared from the clipboard after 2 minutes (on the Mac, while Clippet is running). Clippet asks for these permissions only when you use the feature that needs them: Face ID, Touch ID or Optic ID for App Lock; adding to your photo library for Save to Photos; Contacts for Add to Contacts; Reminders for a Reminder action; and on the Mac, Accessibility for pasting into other apps. The Clippet keyboard needs Allow Full Access to show your clips. It reads them from Clippet's database on the device, never reads what you type in the field, makes no network requests and does not store what you search in it.
