The kanji question

If you tried shogi before and gave up, the board was probably the reason, more than the rules. Every shogi piece is the same pentagonal wedge, told apart only by its characters, so if you can't read them every piece looks the same. Apps handle this in different ways. PiyoShogi and Kakinoki offer simplified or internationalized piece options and expect you to learn by playing. Shogiful (ours) treats it as the central design problem. Its symbolic Western pieces have movement indicators built into each icon and are on by default outside Japan, and the app teaches the traditional kanji step by step while you train. The design story is in our journal post on making shogi readable. Whichever app you choose, you don't need the kanji to start. You will pick them up faster once you know how the pieces move.

A sensible path from zero to rated games

1. Learn the pieces by playing
Start offline against an AI that loses convincingly, such as PiyoShogi's lowest levels or Shogiful's Hana (~500 strength). Play untimed games with no rank at stake. If the kanji stopped you before, use Western pieces first.
2. Build the drop habit
Drops are where chess instincts fail: a piece in hand is an attacking threat that no chess game prepared you for. Tsume (checkmate) puzzles train exactly this. Shogiful schedules them with spaced repetition, and PiyoShogi posts one daily.
3. Understand your losses
Most players skip this step. An evaluation graph won't tell you that you keep weakening your own castle on move 30, but plain-language analysis will. (Shogiful was built for this job, so read this as our opinion.)
4. Then go online
Shogi Wars has official ranks, Lishogi has free open play, and 81Dojo has the international teaching community. Timed games against people are the deep end, and much more fun once you've stopped hanging pieces to drop forks.

Questions

FAQ

What is the best shogi app for iPhone?
It depends on the job. PiyoShogi is the best free all-rounder; Shogiful (ours) is the best for learning and improving in English; Shogi Wars is the best for ranked online play. Most improving players end up with two apps: one to play humans, one to train.
What is the best shogi app for beginners who can't read kanji?
Shogiful is the only one designed around that problem, with Western pieces that show how they move and kanji taught step by step. PiyoShogi and Kakinoki offer internationalized piece options but expect you to learn the pieces as you play.
What is the best free shogi app?
PiyoShogi: 40 graded levels, analysis, and a daily mate problem, all free. For free online play against people, use Lishogi (browser) or ShogiQuest. Shogiful's free tier covers all 10 AI opponents, 2 analyses/day, and 3 puzzles/day.
Which shogi app do Japanese players use?
Mostly Shogi Wars, the official JSA app with recognized kyu/dan ranks. Kanazawa and Kakinoki are the classic play-the-computer apps. For rated games against Japanese players, Shogi Wars or 81Dojo is where that happens.
Can I play shogi offline on iPhone?
Yes. Shogiful (fully, including analysis and training), PiyoShogi's AI play, Kanazawa, and Kakinoki all work offline. The online platforms (Shogi Wars, Lishogi, 81Dojo, ShogiQuest) need a connection.
Should I learn shogi if I already play chess?
Yes. It's the most rewarding next board game for a chess player, because drops break your chess instincts: material never simplifies, and endgames are attacking races. The rules take an evening, but the drop habit takes practice that no chess game gave you.
