Why is bank aggregation the problem?

Automated bill trackers need to see your transactions to work. They ask for read-only API access via Plaid, Yodlee, or similar aggregators. In exchange, you get auto-discovery of subscriptions, bill negotiation, and cashflow insights, all based on the aggregator's view of your finances.

For privacy-conscious people, that trade has real costs:

You're handing over banking credentials
Even with OAuth-style flows, the aggregator holds a token with access to every transaction you make. That covers every transaction, not only bills.
Your data sits on a third-party server
The aggregator caches your transaction history. Any breach at Plaid, Yodlee, or the bill-tracker itself exposes it.
Monetization incentives are mixed
Many bill-negotiation apps take a cut of the savings, so they profit from keeping you engaged, which is not always in your interest.
Your bank may not allow it
Some banks, especially outside the US, don't integrate with aggregators at all. If your bank isn't supported, automated trackers don't work for you.

Isn't manual entry tedious?

Only at the start, and most households can enter every recurring bill in one sitting. After that:

Suggestions fill in the details
Type "Netflix" and AllPaid suggests the category and how often it repeats. The amount is optional, so a bill can be as simple as a name and a due date.
Mark paid is one tap
Do it in the app or straight from the reminder, and undo it if you tapped too fast.
Most bills don't change
Your rent, utilities, and subscriptions are the same every month. Once entered, they recur automatically, so you only add new ones.
The month and the widgets keep it in view
The monthly summary shows the month's bills with due, paid, and remaining totals, and the Monthly Calendar and Coming Up widgets put what's due on your Home Screen, so you rarely need to open the app.

What do you give up with manual bill tracking?

Manual entry means you lose:

  • Automatic subscription discovery (no auto-scan for forgotten recurring charges)
  • Bill negotiation features
  • Cross-account cashflow forecasting
  • The feeling that "the app knows everything about my money"

If you need those features, AllPaid isn't the right choice; an aggregator-based app is, with the privacy trade that comes with it. AllPaid is for people who would rather spend one sitting on setup and keep their bank credentials to themselves.
