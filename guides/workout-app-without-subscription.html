Why is subscription-only workout pricing a real problem?

Workout logging suits a one-time purchase: it is simple in scope, infrequently updated, and used in bursts of 45 to 90 minutes. Saving a set of numbers does not require a recurring server bill.

Even so, the biggest workout loggers put their core features behind a paid tier, and most bill for it monthly or yearly. Prices below are from the US App Store, checked 29 October 2026:

Strong
Freemium. Strong PRO is $4.99 a month or $23.99 to $29.99 a year, and Strong PRO Forever is a lifetime purchase ($79.99 and $99.99 are both listed). Multiple routines, progress tracking, and cloud sync are behind the paywall. Many lifters treat it as the baseline strength logger, and many also find it overpriced for what it does.
Hevy
The free tier covers a lot at first, but advanced features and some export functions need Hevy Pro: $2.99 or $3.99 a month, $23.99 a year, or $74.99 as a lifetime purchase.
Fitbod
AI-generated workouts on a subscription only: $12.99 to $15.99 a month or $79.99 to $95.99 a year, with no lifetime option. It works, and it costs more per month than Strong or Hevy.
Caliber
A coaching-style app with personalized programs. Caliber Plus is monthly or yearly only, with no lifetime option, and its price pays for human-coach overhead more than for the app.
