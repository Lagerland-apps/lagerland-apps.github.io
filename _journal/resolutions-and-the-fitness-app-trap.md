It's the third of January and the fitness apps are already doing the thing.

The thing starts with a gentle, well-designed, nicely typeset push notification saying you missed your goal by *just a little*, which is a shame, but you can still recover if you log in *right now*. After that comes a streak counter that resets if you don't open the app for two days, a leaderboard against three friends you haven't seen since college, a coach character with a frowning face, and a colour change from green to amber to red as the days slip.

I'm not making fun of these apps. Talented people design them, the typography and animations are good, and they convert. They are doing exactly what their growth dashboards reward. They are not helping you train.

## The mechanism

If a fitness app has ever turned on you in week two, this is what was happening on the engineering side:

1. You signed up because you wanted to do something more consistently.
2. The app measured "consistency" with a streak counter, a single integer that resets to zero on any miss.
3. Streaks are sticky in the short term. People do open the app to keep them alive.
4. Streaks are also brittle: miss one day and the counter resets. The app's growth team knows this, and it's why streaks work. The fragility keeps you opening the app.
5. So when you miss day four, the app punishes you, you feel bad, and one of two things happens. You panic-open and log a fake workout to save the streak, or you slide into feeling bad and quietly stop opening the app for the rest of the year.

You didn't train in either case. One is theatre and the other is a churned user. Both feel like the app's fault, and they kind of are.

## What does a non-hostile fitness app look like?

A handful of design choices reliably mark an app that will still treat you well in week three:

- **No streak counter on the home screen.** If there is one, it doesn't reset to zero. It counts total days trained over your lifetime and never goes down, so missing Tuesday costs you nothing.
- **Follow-ups for missed work.** Tasks or workouts that didn't happen yesterday aren't punished or marked red. The app offers them to you again today. They roll forward and nothing resets.
- **No comparison with people you didn't choose.** Leaderboards are off by default, and there are no friends whose routines you're pushed to compete with.
- **Notifications that explain themselves.** "You haven't logged in three days" is hostile. "Your last lifting session was Thursday. Here's the program for tomorrow if you want it" uses the same data and treats you like an adult.
- **A way to leave for a week without losing your data.** People go on holiday, get injured, get busy. The app should be where you left it when you come back.

## What this studio ships

[Taskful Day](/apps/taskful-day/) is a daily planner with no streak counter, flame, or red X. Unfinished tasks roll forward to tomorrow. There is a focus-session timer and a reflective view if you want them, and nothing pretends to know whether you had a "good day" or a "bad day."

[GymLogger X](/apps/gymlogger-x/) is a strength-training log without a social feed, leaderboard, or streak guilt. It tracks sets, reps, PRs, and 1RM trends. If you skip a week, the app does not turn red.

Fitness apps don't have to be flat and joyless. Pacing and motivation take real craft, and so does the small celebration when you hit a personal record. My complaint is with a baseline that runs on guilt. A celebration should come from what the user achieved, and the app shouldn't squeeze it out of them by punishing the rest of their life.

## A small ask for the third of January

If you set yourself a fitness target for 2026, the most useful advice I have is to **pick the app that treats you well in week three over the one that converts hardest in week one**. They are usually different apps, and the second kind will hurt you in February.

That's the whole post. Go for a walk, lift something, and don't let the streak counter run your life.
