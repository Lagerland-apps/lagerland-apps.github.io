  - q: "How is AppMeta Pulse different from the official App Store Connect app?"
    a: "Apple's <a href=\"https://apps.apple.com/us/app/app-store-connect/id1234793120\" rel=\"noopener\">App Store Connect app</a> is a full management tool — it can submit builds, reply to reviews, manage TestFlight, change metadata. AppMeta Pulse is the opposite: a focused dashboard built for quick monitoring rather than management. Apart from the analytics report requests Apple needs, the only thing it sends to App Store Connect is a review reply you write. Faster to launch, simpler to read, no chance of accidentally pushing a build at the coffee shop."

  - q: "What's the difference between sales and proceeds in App Store Connect?"
    a: "<em>Sales</em> is the gross amount customers paid before Apple's commission. <em>Proceeds</em> is what Apple owes you after its 15% (Small Business Program) or 30% (standard) cut and any VAT or sales tax it collects. The payment that reaches your bank can still differ from your proceeds, because of currency conversion, withholding tax, and bank fees. AppMeta Pulse displays proceeds by default — the Sales and Trends figure closest to what you get paid — and lets you switch to gross sales in Settings → Display when you want to compare against Sales and Trends in the App Store Connect web UI. See Apple's reference on <a href=\"https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/view-sales-and-trends/view-units-proceeds-sales-and-pre-orders/\" rel=\"noopener\">units, proceeds, and sales</a> for the official definitions."

  - q: "Does AppMeta Pulse use the App Store Connect API or the web dashboard?"
    a: "The official <a href=\"https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appstoreconnectapi\" rel=\"noopener\">App Store Connect API</a>. You generate an API key with the Admin role in App Store Connect → Users and Access → Integrations, download the .p8 private key, and import the key file + Key ID + Issuer ID into AppMeta Pulse, along with your Vendor Number for the sales reports. The key material is stored in your system Keychain. AppMeta Pulse never scrapes the web dashboard. See <a href=\"/apps/appmeta-pulse/api-access/\">the API access methodology page</a> for the exact endpoints called, the role you need on the key, and the JWT lifecycle."

  - q: "What permissions does AppMeta Pulse request on my App Store Connect account?"
    a: "AppMeta Pulse asks for an API key with the Admin role. Admin is the role that covers sales reports, App Analytics, and review replies together; App Manager and Developer keys can't read sales reports, and Apple answers them with a 403 error. It uses the key to read your sales and subscription reports, your app catalog, App Store analytics, and customer reviews. The only things it writes are the review replies you choose to send and the analytics report requests Apple needs to prepare impressions data; it has no pricing, build, or metadata endpoints. You can revoke the key in App Store Connect at any time without uninstalling the app. <a href=\"/apps/appmeta-pulse/api-access/\">Full role-to-endpoint mapping is documented on the methodology page.</a>"

  - q: "How fresh is the data — is it real-time?"
    a: "It is as fresh as Apple makes available. Apple publishes each day's Sales and Trends report the following day, generally by 8 a.m. Pacific Time, and the daily subscription reports follow the same schedule; App Store analytics such as impressions arrive a day or two later. AppMeta Pulse checks in the background about hourly until the new day lands (iOS decides the exact timing), and again when you open the app or pull to refresh; it cannot make Apple's data faster than Apple does. If a number looks stuck, it is almost always Apple's reporting cadence, not the app."

Apple's own App Store Connect app is built to <em>manage</em> a developer business — submit builds, reply to reviews, edit metadata, manage TestFlight. That is the right tool for the work. It is not the right tool for the question almost every indie publisher actually asks ten times a day: <em>how is yesterday looking?</em> Open the official app on a phone and you are three taps and a loading spinner away from a number you wanted in two seconds.

AppMeta Pulse was built to be the other half — the monitoring-first, calm dashboard for the daily glance. Open the app, see the latest day's proceeds, MRR, active subscriptions, trial conversions, and downloads across every app you ship. Drill into one app when something looks off. Compare this week to last week. Close the app. Get back to building.

The limits are not a marketing line; they are in the code. AppMeta Pulse has no code for submitting builds, changing pricing, or editing metadata. The only things it writes to App Store Connect are the review replies you choose to send and the analytics report requests Apple needs to prepare impressions data. The .p8 private key and key ID live in your system Keychain and never sync, even with iCloud Sync on. Queries hit Apple's API servers directly over HTTPS; nothing is routed through Lagerland Apps servers, and the App Store privacy label declares zero data collection — verifiable on the listing, not just an asserted line of copy.

The dashboard uses Apple's own vocabulary: <em>proceeds</em> (after Apple's 15% Small Business or 30% standard cut and country VAT), <em>sales</em> (gross before commission), <em>units</em>, <em>active subscriptions</em>, <em>trial conversions</em>, <em>cancellations</em>. If you have ever had to translate a third-party dashboard's relabelled numbers back into Apple's terms, Pulse skips that step. Its figures come from Sales and Trends, so they are next-day numbers, not the final amounts in your monthly <a href="https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/getting-paid/download-financial-reports/" rel="noopener">financial reports</a>.
